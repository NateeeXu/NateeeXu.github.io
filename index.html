Every screen, explained

straight from the app

  • Home and role-based entry points

    The home page brings together campus-based search and two paths: finding a home and listing a sublet. Area listings, recommendations and housing requests help users enter the relevant browsing or publishing flow.

  • Listing creation and information entry

    A step-by-step form collects the apartment, campus, address, area, transfer type and pricing. Apartment matching and address-completion guidance help organize listing information, with options to save a draft or continue.

  • Listings and search filters

    Listings are organized by the selected campus and shuttle routes, with rent, dates, area and verification status shown together. Search, favorites and filters support browsing, alongside an entry point for a content-submission campaign.

  • Listing details and inquiry entry points

    The detail page presents photos, pricing, dates, address, layout and shuttle coverage for one listing. Users can save the listing, view its location, share it or start an in-app inquiry.

  • Housing requests and notifications

    Housing-request cards show the campus, budget, move-in dates and preferred layout, helping listing owners understand demand. Users can filter requests, open details and enable notifications for new requests.

  • In-app inquiries and contact exchange

    The chat connects an inquiry to its listing and displays conversation history and message status. The contact-exchange area shows the number of conversation rounds and the other user’s identity-verification status.

  • Sharing copy and campaign entry points

    Listing information is formatted as sharing copy for WeChat groups or RedNote. Users can choose a writing style, preview the content and copy the title and body. The page also includes a reminder to add genuine experience and a campaign entry point.

  • Campus transit and listings along shuttle routes

    The map displays campus shuttle routes, vehicle online status and location markers, with controls to switch routes. A linked list of homes along the routes lets users consider commuting options while browsing.

  • Local living guides

    A series of local guides covers topics such as seasonal clothing and nearby cafes and dessert shops. Expandable articles provide information about everyday life around campus beyond the housing search.

  • User profile and verification

    The profile shows the user’s avatar, student-verification status, join date, listings and housing requests. Profile editing and privacy settings sit alongside a consolidated view of the user’s published content.

  • Account center and service management

    The account center brings together listings, housing requests, favorites and browsing history. It also provides access to notifications, contact and privacy settings, review status, rewards, feedback and customer support.

Why I built it

Students were hunting for sublets in fragmented group chats and social feeds: campuses mixed together, fields inconsistent, and phone numbers exposed too early. I defined the positioning, PRD, information architecture, and data model, then built it myself as a WeChat Mini Program on Tencent CloudBase. I also built 3 user communities at Northwestern, Yale, and UC Berkeley, now 300+ members in total.

Problem framing

Campus as context

Schools, neighborhoods, apartment aliases, and shuttle routes became one discovery system, so a listing is always read in the context of the campus it serves.

Trust by design

Contact comes last

Contact details are exchanged only after both sides have actually talked. The platform stays out of rent, deposits, and contracts, and the product says so plainly.

Beta discipline

Two rounds, measured

Ran two beta rounds with 80 users and 122 test scenarios. I diagnosed and shipped every fix myself, resolving 13 issues from the second round, then launched on WeChat in November 2026.
